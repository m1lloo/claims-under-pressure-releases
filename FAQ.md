# 🎮 General

## What is Claims: Under Pressure?

**Claims: Under Pressure** is an insurance claims management simulation game.

You are responsible for running a busy claims operation where new cases continuously arrive and compete for your attention.

You'll need to:

- Triage incoming claims
- Decide which cases need attention first
- Assign claims to members of your team
- Monitor staff workloads
- Review claim information
- Request evidence
- Investigate suspicious cases
- Make important claim decisions
- Handle complaints and escalations
- Manage increasing operational pressure
- Keep the department moving while new work continues to arrive

The game is designed around managing the **whole operation**, rather than simply processing one claim at a time.

# 🏢 Gameplay

## What do I actually do?

You manage an insurance claims operation.

New claims arrive throughout the simulation and initially wait for **your triage**.

You'll review the information available, determine the priority of the case and then assign it to an appropriate member of your team.

Your staff handle routine work while you continue managing the wider operation.

Claims may later return to your attention when:

- Evidence arrives
- A decision is required
- Something suspicious is discovered
- A complaint occurs
- An escalation happens
- Further investigation is required
- New information changes the case

Meanwhile, more claims continue arriving.

---

## Do I personally triage every claim?

Yes.

Initial claim triage is one of the player's main responsibilities.

New claims enter the **Awaiting Triage** queue.

You decide how urgently the claim needs attention and get it into the right hands.

Your handlers cannot simply clear the entire incoming queue without you.

If claims start arriving faster than you can triage them, the queue can grow.

That's part of the pressure.

---

## Who accepts or rejects claims: me or the handler?

**You make the important claim decisions.**

Handlers perform much of the routine work between your decisions, but they don't replace you as the person running the operation.

When a case reaches a point where an important decision is required, you can review the information and decide what happens next.

Depending on the claim, your options may include:

- Accepting the claim
- Partially accepting the claim
- Rejecting the claim
- Requesting additional evidence
- Referring the claim for investigation
- Authorising certain forms of assistance

The exact options available can depend on the case and its current stage.

---

## What do handlers do?

Handlers are the people doing much of the day-to-day work on the claims you assign to them.

You:

1. Receive the claim
2. Triage it
3. Determine its priority
4. Assign it

Your team then handles routine work while simulation time progresses.

You'll become involved again when something requires your attention.

This means you are managing an operation rather than manually performing every action that every employee would perform.

---

## Can handlers become overloaded?

Yes.

Workload management is part of the game.

Assigning everything to the same person isn't necessarily going to make the queue disappear.

You need to consider things such as:

- Existing workloads
- Availability
- Capacity
- Department
- Current operational pressure

An overloaded team can contribute to delays elsewhere in the operation.

---

## Do I investigate claims myself?

You are involved in investigations and important decisions, but you don't personally perform every routine task.

You can review claim information and request evidence when required.

Cases may involve things such as:

- Customer statements
- Third-party statements
- Photographs
- Receipts
- Repair estimates
- Specialist reports
- Claim history
- Policy information
- Inconsistencies
- Fraud indicators

Some of this information can take simulation time to become available.

---

## Is every suspicious claim fraudulent?

No.

This is important.

**Suspicious does not automatically mean fraudulent.**

A legitimate claim can contain unusual information.

Someone can make a mistake.

Evidence can initially appear inconsistent.

Circumstances can genuinely be unusual.

Part of the challenge is deciding when something deserves additional investigation without treating every slightly unusual case as fraud.

---

## What types of claims are in the game?

The game includes several claim categories, including:

### 🚗 Motor

Examples can include collisions, theft, vandalism, weather damage and vehicle losses.

### 🏠 Home

Examples can include flooding, fires, burglary, storms, escape of water and accidental damage.

### ✈️ Travel

Examples can include cancellations, delays, lost baggage, theft and emergency expenses.

### 🐾 Pet

Examples can include illness, injury, diagnostics, surgery and emergency treatment.

### 📱 Gadget

Examples can include theft, accidental damage, cracked screens and liquid damage.

### 🚨 Major Loss

Larger and more complicated incidents that can require significantly more attention and resources.

---

## Are claims randomly generated?

The game uses generated claim scenarios and simulation systems to create a varied workload.

Claims can differ in their circumstances, information, complexity and underlying truth.

You shouldn't assume that two apparently similar claims will necessarily have the same outcome.

---

## Can claims last multiple in-game days?

Yes.

Not every claim is resolved immediately.

Some cases can remain open while:

- Evidence is requested
- Reports are prepared
- Investigations continue
- Repairs progress
- New information arrives
- Complaints are handled
- Decisions are reviewed

A complicated case can remain part of your workload across multiple simulated days.

---

## Can things happen to a claim after I've dealt with it?

Depending on the claim and its lifecycle, new developments can occur while a case remains active.

A claim isn't necessarily finished just because you've performed one action.

That's part of the simulation.

# 📈 Pressure & Management

## What does the pressure system do?

The operation can move through different levels of pressure depending on what's happening.

These include:

- **NORMAL**
- **BUSY**
- **VERY BUSY**
- **SEVERE PRESSURE**

Pressure reflects conditions across the operation rather than simply being a cosmetic difficulty label.

Things such as growing queues, workloads, urgent cases, outstanding work and other operational problems can contribute to pressure.

---

## Can the triage queue become overloaded?

Yes.

Claims continue arriving while simulation time progresses.

If you're not keeping up with incoming work, your **Awaiting Triage** queue can grow.

That can then delay claims reaching handlers.

---

## Is there a simulation clock?

Yes.

The game runs using simulation time.

Available controls include:

- Pause
- 1×
- 5×
- 15×
- 60×

Higher speeds allow you to progress through quieter periods more quickly.

They can also allow problems to develop much faster if you're not paying attention.

Use `60×` responsibly. 🙂

---

## Does the game continue while I'm looking at a claim?

Unless the simulation is paused, the wider operation continues running.

That's intentional.

While you're focused on one case:

- Another claim might arrive
- Evidence might return
- A handler might complete work
- A complaint might appear
- Another case might need attention

You can pause the simulation when you need time to think.

# 💾 Saves

## Does the game save my operation?

Yes.

Claims: Under Pressure uses local saves to preserve your operation.

Your active simulation can be continued later.

---

## Where are my saves stored?

Save data is stored locally using the application's user data directory.

The game maintains files such as its primary save, backup data and settings within its local application data.

The exact location can vary depending on the Windows environment.

---

## Does the game have a backup save?

The game includes backup handling intended to reduce the risk of losing an operation if the primary save encounters a problem.

However, no save system can guarantee protection against every possible problem.

---

## Should I back up saves when using beta versions?

**Yes.**

Beta builds are experimental.

If you are participating in beta testing and have an operation you care about, keeping your own backup is strongly recommended.

Beta versions may change systems or save structures during development.

## Will beta saves work in later versions?

Compatibility is something we try to preserve where practical, but **beta save compatibility cannot always be guaranteed**.

A beta version may introduce changes that require migration or make older experimental saves unsuitable.

If a particular beta has known save compatibility concerns, testers will be informed where possible.

---

## Will my beta progress carry into the release version?

Do not assume that beta progress will become your permanent release save.

Beta builds exist for testing.

If preserving a beta save into the final release is supported, this will be communicated separately.

# 🖥️ Performance & Troubleshooting

## The game is running slowly. What should I do?

If you experience poor performance:

1. Record your game version.
2. Note approximately how large/busy your operation was.
3. Note whether the problem occurs at a particular simulation speed.
4. Include your basic system specifications.
5. Submit a report if the issue is repeatable.

Performance issues that only occur after a simulation has been running for a long time are particularly useful to report.

---

## Something looks broken after updating.

Try completely closing and reopening the game first.

If the issue continues, report:

- Previous version
- Current version
- Whether it happens on an existing save
- Whether it happens on a new operation
- Screenshots where possible

That information can help determine whether the issue is related to the update, save migration or the UI itself.

---

## My save won't load.

Don't immediately delete your save files.

If possible:

1. Stop making changes to the affected save.
2. Record the version that created it.
3. Record the version you're currently running.
4. Report the problem.

A backup may exist that can help with recovery.

---

## Should I reinstall if something breaks?

Reinstalling can sometimes resolve damaged application files, but it shouldn't be the first response to every problem.

If the problem appears to involve your operation/save, reinstalling the application may not solve it.

Report reproducible problems so they can actually be investigated.

## What's the difference between a Release and a Pre-release?

A **Release** is intended for normal stable distribution.

A **Pre-release** is an experimental build intended for testing before a stable version is ready.

For example:

`v1.2.0`

would represent a stable release.

While:

`v1.2.0-beta.1`

would represent a beta build of the upcoming version.

---

## Should normal players install Pre-releases?

Only if you deliberately want to test experimental software.

Pre-releases may contain bugs or unfinished changes.

If you just want to play the game normally, use the latest stable release.

---

## Why are there multiple beta versions?

Software testing is iterative.

For example:

`beta.1`

might introduce a new feature.

Testing then discovers problems.

Those problems are fixed in:

`beta.2`

Further testing might produce:

`beta.3`

Once the update is considered ready, it can become the normal stable release.

---

## Why isn't the newest GitHub version appearing in my updater?

Check whether the newest version is marked as a **Pre-release**.

Experimental pre-release builds aren't intended to be automatically sent to normal stable installations.

The latest stable release remains the normal update target.
